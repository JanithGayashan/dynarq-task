# Research

These notes use only the two supplied files in `sources/`. They summarize the
provided directive extract for the owner and are not legal advice.

1. How does the directive define statutory interest for late payment?

   Answer: Statutory interest for late payment is **simple interest** at a rate
   equal to the reference rate **plus at least eight percentage points**. For a
   euro Member State, the reference rate is the ECB rate applied to its most
   recent main refinancing operations, or the marginal rate from the most recent
   variable-rate tender for those operations. For a non-euro Member State, it is
   the equivalent rate set by its national central bank.

   Source: [Source 1, Article 2(6) and 2(7)(a)-(b)](sources/late-payment-directive.md#article-2-definitions).

2. The owner's note says the EU maximum late fee is 25 EUR. Is that right?

   Answer: **No; the supplied directive does not support that claim.** It says
   that when late-payment interest becomes payable under Article 3 or 4, the
   creditor is entitled to a **minimum fixed sum of EUR 40** as compensation for
   its own recovery costs. It also provides for reasonable compensation for
   recovery costs exceeding that fixed sum and incurred because of late payment.
   This is recovery-cost compensation, not a stated general maximum late fee of
   EUR 25. The owner's requested wording is therefore unsupported by these files.

   Sources: [Source 1, Article 6(1)-(3)](sources/late-payment-directive.md#article-6-compensation-for-recovery-costs);
   [Source 2, item 1](sources/owner-note.md), which records the owner's unchecked belief.

3. Does the business have to send a reminder before it is entitled to interest and to
   the fixed compensation sum?

   Answer: **No reminder is required.** In transactions between businesses,
   Article 3 provides entitlement to late-payment interest without a reminder
   when the creditor has fulfilled its contractual and legal obligations and
   has not received the amount due on time, unless the debtor is not responsible
   for the delay. The fixed compensation sum is available where late-payment
   interest becomes payable under Article 3 or 4; Article 6 expressly says that
   this sum is payable without a reminder. Entitlement therefore depends on the
   stated conditions, rather than on the business sending a reminder first.

   Sources: [Source 1, Article 3(1)(a)-(b)](sources/late-payment-directive.md#article-3-transactions-between-undertakings)
   and [Article 6(1)-(2)](sources/late-payment-directive.md#article-6-compensation-for-recovery-costs).

4. What is the current ECB reference rate?

   Answer: **Not in the sources.** The directive defines the relevant ECB-based
   rate but does not provide a current numerical value. The owner's note asks
   for the rate without supplying it. To answer, we would need a dated official
   ECB publication giving the rate for its most recent main refinancing
   operations, or the applicable marginal rate from its variable-rate tender.
   For calculating interest between businesses, we would also need the rate in
   force on the relevant 1 January or 1 July: Article 3 fixes the applicable rate
   for each half of the year using those dates. A current rate should not be
   guessed from these files.

   Sources: [Source 1, Article 2(7)(a)](sources/late-payment-directive.md#article-2-definitions)
   and [Article 3(2)(a)-(b)](sources/late-payment-directive.md#article-3-transactions-between-undertakings);
   [Source 2, item 4](sources/owner-note.md).
